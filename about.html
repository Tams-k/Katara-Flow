The problem

Much of Nairobi's drainage was built decades ago and has not kept pace with the growth of the city. Many drains are too small, poorly maintained or blocked by solid waste, especially in informal and low-income areas. When it rains hard, the result is flooding that keeps coming back.

Residents can already tell the authorities about a faulty drain, but they seldom see what happens next. Without that feedback, they stop trusting the process and stop reporting, and NCWSC loses the location information it needs to find the worst trouble spots.

The gap is not that no reporting channel exists. It is that residents cannot see that their report led anywhere.

Our aim

To design and build a drainage reporting and tracking system in which residents report blocked or badly kept drains, and then track each report as NCWSC updates it with feedback.

Specific objectives

  1. Assess the current state of drainage infrastructure management in urban areas and the challenges involved.
  2. Examine existing systems used to manage drainage and similar public reports.
  3. Design a system that lets residents report a drainage problem and follow its progress.
  4. Develop it as a working piece of software.
  5. Test it against what residents need.

Scope and limits

  • In scope

    Report submission with a location tag, the three status stages, and notifications that tell the resident when the status changes. Nairobi only, and drainage problems only.

  • Out of scope

    The physical repair of drains, which stays with NCWSC, and other complaints such as electricity, waste collection or roads.

  • Known limits

    Not everyone has a smartphone or a steady connection, so the interface is kept light and does not assume a smartphone. Resolution is confirmed by NCWSC inside the system rather than by separate field checks.

Goals we support

Katara Flow contributes to Sustainable Development Goals 6 (clean water and sanitation), 11 (sustainable cities and communities) and 13 (climate action), by making urban infrastructure safer and more accountable.

At a glance

  • Every report moves through three stages: Reported, In progress and Resolved.
  • Katara Flow covers drainage problems in Nairobi only. It does not cover roads, waste collection or electricity.
  • Built to be light, so it works on a slow connection. Report a drain.
